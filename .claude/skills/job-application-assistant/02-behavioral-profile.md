# Behavioral Profile

## Overview
Synthesized from self-assessment interview. Profile type: **Analytical Autonomous**. Strong preference for deep research, evidence-based work and written communication; performs best in fast-paced environments with clear outcomes and low political friction.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Research orientation | High | Energised by deep analysis, complex problems, finding the "why" behind things |
| Autonomy | High | Works well independently; doesn't require close supervision or constant collaboration |
| Deliberateness | High | Gathers data and builds evidence-based arguments before acting; avoids impulsive decisions |
| Collaboration | Moderate | Adapts well to teamwork but has operated largely independently in practice |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Research & synthesis:** Enjoys diving deep into complex topics, synthesising large amounts of information into coherent, evidence-based arguments
- **Written communication:** Strongest in written form — memos, reports, policy analysis — rather than verbal debate or live advocacy
- **Analytical problem-solving:** Naturally builds structured arguments from evidence; comfortable with regulatory complexity and ambiguity
- **Technology adoption:** Proactively integrates AI tools and digital workflows to improve efficiency and output quality

## How You Work Best
- Fast-paced environments with analytical demands and clear deliverables
- Roles that reward depth of thinking and quality of written output
- Work that produces tangible or meaningful outcomes (not just internal bureaucracy)
- Autonomy to structure and execute research without micromanagement
- Collaborative when needed, but comfortable driving work independently

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Adversarial settings:** Less suited to high-conflict, politically charged environments. Frame as: "I prefer outcomes-focused environments where decisions are made on evidence rather than politics."
- **Verbal advocacy:** Stronger in written than spoken argumentation. Frame as: "I bring rigour to written analysis and prepare thoroughly for any oral presentations."

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "Research", "analysis", "evidence-based", "policy development"
- "Autonomous", "self-directed", "independent"
- "Written reports", "briefings", "technical writing"
- "Complex", "multi-stakeholder", "cross-functional"
- "Fast-paced", "deadline-driven", "statutory timeframes"
- "GIS", "spatial", "data-informed"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not a deal-breaker, but worth discussing):
- "Sales", "business development", "client acquisition"
- "Negotiation", "advocacy", "lobbying"
- "High volume of public-facing interactions"
- "Fast decision-making with limited information" (prefers deliberate, evidence-based approach)

## Management Style Preferences
- Values managers who set clear goals and trust their team to deliver
- Does not thrive under micromanagement or in environments where decisions are made without evidence
- Appreciates feedback on written outputs and clear communication about priorities

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Lead with research depth and analytical rigour; reference the ability to translate complexity into clear, evidence-based outputs for decision-makers
- **CV:** Emphasise policy analysis, comparative research, written briefings, and evidence-based recommendations; highlight AI tool fluency and GIS interest
- **Interviews:** Use Plan Change 78 and comparative policy research as primary STAR examples; frame the move away from planning as a deliberate, forward-looking career decision
- **Don't overstate:** Avoid claiming strong verbal advocacy, sales comfort, or high-volume public engagement as strengths
