# Interview Preparation Guide

## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. Plan Change 78 — City Centre Provisions (Leadership, Project Management, Policy Development)
**S:** Auckland Council needed to update the Auckland Unitary Plan to address growth, development and urban design in the city centre. Plan Change 78 was a major statutory plan change — a complex, multi-year process with significant implications for how Auckland's city centre would develop.
**T:** As one of the lead planners for the city centre workstream, I was responsible for developing the planning provisions from initial concept through to the change being made operative.
**A:** Led strategic research and policy investigations; developed and tested planning provisions including land use rules, urban design standards and precinct policies. Conducted comparative analysis of approaches in other NZ and international cities. Coordinated with technical specialists, consultants, elected members and decision-makers. Managed the programme to statutory timeframes across a multi-party process including consultant procurement and technical input coordination.
**R:** Plan Change 78 progressed from conceptual development through to made operative, delivering a new planning framework for Auckland's city centre — a significant statutory outcome for New Zealand's largest city.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a complex project you led", "Give me an example of managing multiple stakeholders", "Describe a time you drove a significant outcome from start to finish", "Tell me about a time you managed competing priorities"

### 2. Comparative International Policy Research (Research, Analysis, Synthesis)
**S:** New Zealand's planning legislation has undergone significant reform in recent years, including new national policy statements requiring councils to fundamentally reassess their planning approaches. Auckland Council needed to understand how other cities and jurisdictions had responded to similar policy challenges.
**T:** I was responsible for conducting comparative policy research across New Zealand and international cities to assess different planning approaches and inform policy recommendations.
**A:** Identified relevant comparable cities and policy contexts; researched planning frameworks, outcomes and lessons learned across multiple jurisdictions. Synthesised findings into structured analytical outputs — written briefings, memos and presentations — tailored for technical specialists and senior decision-makers. Used AI tools including Claude to accelerate research and drafting workflows.
**R:** Delivered evidence-based comparative analysis that directly informed policy development and the Council's response to legislative reform, ensuring Auckland's approach was grounded in international best practice.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you had to analyse complex or ambiguous information", "Describe your research process", "How do you handle large volumes of information?", "Give me an example of presenting complex findings to senior stakeholders"

---

### 3. Precinct Masterplanning and Scenario Planning (Analytical, Spatial, Technical)
**S:** Before committing to planning policy positions for specific city centre precincts, the Council needed to test whether its policy directions would actually produce the urban design outcomes it intended — particularly around density, built form and public realm.
**T:** I was responsible for delivering precinct masterplanning and scenario planning to test different built form, density and urban design configurations against policy objectives.
**A:** Developed multiple planning scenarios for key precincts; used spatial analysis and ArcGIS to test how different configurations performed against design and policy criteria. Prepared visual and written materials to communicate findings to technical specialists and decision-makers. Iterated scenarios in response to stakeholder feedback to refine the policy approach.
**R:** Provided the spatial evidence base for land use strategy and policy development, ensuring the Council's planning provisions were tested against real-world built form outcomes before adoption.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you used data or analysis to support a recommendation", "Describe your approach to problem-solving", "Give an example of translating technical analysis into decision-ready outputs"

---

### 4. Complex Resource Consent Assessment — Contested Case (Regulatory Complexity, Decision-Making)
**S:** As an Intermediate Planner, I regularly assessed development applications involving overlapping statutory frameworks, including heritage works, coastal permits and contaminated land — some of which were contested by applicants or objectors.
**T:** Required to deliver consistent, evidence-based planning recommendations in complex regulatory contexts, often with competing interests and time pressure.
**A:** Applied and interpreted multiple overlapping statutory frameworks (Auckland Unitary Plan, RMA, coastal policy); coordinated multidisciplinary technical inputs; negotiated solutions with applicants, consultants and specialists where proposals had issues. Prepared comprehensive planning assessments with clear evidence-based recommendations.
**R:** Delivered robust planning outcomes across a high volume of complex cases, including contested matters — demonstrating the ability to navigate regulatory complexity and reach defensible, evidence-based conclusions under pressure.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you had to make a difficult decision", "Describe a situation where you had to balance competing interests", "Give me an example of working under pressure with a deadline", "Tell me about a time you dealt with conflict"

### 5. AI Tool Integration for Workflow Improvement (Initiative, Technology, Productivity)
**S:** Policy planning involves processing large volumes of documents, legislation, research and technical reports. The traditional approach — reading everything manually, drafting from scratch — was time-consuming and left limited time for higher-value analytical work.
**T:** I wanted to find ways to improve my personal productivity and the quality of my analytical outputs without compromising accuracy or rigour.
**A:** Proactively integrated AI tools — Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT, Gemini — into my research, drafting and review workflows. Used them to summarise lengthy documents, generate first-draft structures, identify gaps in arguments, create presentation materials and build personal productivity tools (templates, calculators, project timelines) using AI-assisted coding.
**R:** Meaningfully improved the speed and quality of my outputs. Developed a practical, working understanding of how AI tools can augment professional workflows — now a genuine differentiator in applications to tech-adjacent or analytical roles.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you showed initiative", "How do you keep up with new tools and technologies?", "Give me an example of improving a process", "What's your approach to professional development?"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why are you leaving planning / Auckland Council?"
> Planning has become increasingly politicised and subject to frequent legislative change in New Zealand — the environment has shifted away from the evidence-based, outcomes-focused work I find most engaging. After 10+ years I've developed strong transferable skills, and I'm deliberately looking for a role where I can apply my research and analytical strengths in a new sector and context, ideally internationally. I'm also actively developing my GIS skills with a view to formal study, and I want a role that complements that direction.

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience]."
> Acknowledge directly, then bridge: "You're right that I haven't worked in [sector] before, but what I do bring is [specific transferable skill] — in planning I [concrete example]. I'm a fast learner with a strong research background, and I've already been proactively developing [adjacent skill, e.g. GIS] through self-directed study."

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> I'm planning to study geospatial science/GIS formally within the next couple of years, so in five years I'd like to be in a role that sits at the intersection of spatial analysis, policy or research, and technology. I'm interested in growing my technical depth while continuing to use the analytical and communication skills I've built. International experience is part of that picture too.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> I'm more effective in analytical and written work than in adversarial settings — I do my best work when decisions are made on evidence rather than through debate. I've learned to work in high-stakes, contested environments (planning is inherently that), but I'm deliberately seeking roles where rigour of analysis is valued over ability to win arguments.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"
- "How much of the work is independent analysis vs. collaborative delivery?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how is work typically structured?"
- "What backgrounds do people in the team come from?"
- "How do you onboard people who are transitioning from a different sector?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "How does the team use data and spatial tools in its work?"
- "Is there room to grow into more strategic or analytical responsibilities over time?"
- "How does the team approach professional development and upskilling?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between independent work and collaboration?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here tend to have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
